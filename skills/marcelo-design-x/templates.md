# MDX Templates by Archetype

Ten cloneable site templates derived from Marcelo's real client work (mdx.so, Drip Aqua, 73 Strings, Flight Path) and his Behance portfolio (concept/spec work for Nike, Red Bull, Logitech, plus full launches for Cirus Web3, Swanson Reserve, Future Mortgage, OTT, DMB, Lapin, Fedorgost). Each template gives you: section order, layout patterns, copy archetype, color/typography defaults, and the 3+ custom moments that defined the original site. **Pick one. Don't mix.**

When you clone a template, you still run all 6 phases of `SKILL.md`. The template is your starting scaffold, not a substitute for the thesis or the slop audit.

# § A — Agency / Studio Template

**Reference:** mdx.so
**Use for:** multi-service creative agency, dev shop, design studio, branding agency
**Feeling defaults:** *anticipation, warmth, confidence*

## A.1 — Section order

1. Loader (5-line cycling copy, counter, progress bar)
2. Navbar — asymmetric three-region with vertical divider and custom SVG hover (the wave button)
3. Hero — two-clause emotional headline, blur atmosphere behind, single CTA
4. Service grid — 4 cards (UI/UX · Development · Branding · 3D Animation, or your equivalents)
5. Philosophy / "every experience begins with a feeling" beat
6. Showreel — Vimeo lightbox with custom controls
7. Filterable project gallery — pills + GSAP Flip
8. Process / about block (optional)
9. Contact — emotional hook + service-interest checkboxes + character-roll send
10. Footer — wistful one-liner + email + socials + legal + scroll-top

## A.2 — Color & typography

- Base: `#0A0A0A`
- Off-white: `#F5F2EC` (warm)
- Signal: agency-defining accent (mdx.so uses `#FF8200` orange)
- Mute: `#5B6871`
- Sans: Aventa or Geist Sans
- Display: Orlean or Cabinet Grotesk

## A.3 — Custom moments (use ≥4)

- ✦ Wave-draw SVG hover in navbar
- ✦ Loader copy-mask cycler (5 stacked lines, vertical scroll on counter tick)
- ✦ Character-roll CTA hover on every primary button
- ✦ GSAP Flip filtering on the project gallery
- ✦ Vimeo lightbox with custom chrome (play/pause, scrubber, mute, close)
- ✦ Two-bar SVG close-X for menu

## A.4 — Copy archetype

- Hero: `[Verb] That [Feels]. [Noun] That [Resonates].` — two-clause structure, parallel grammar
- Service grid eyebrow: short, all-caps, tracking-wide labels
- Philosophy section: one short emotional headline, one paragraph
- Contact hook: declarative aspirational sentence, not a question
- Footer one-liner: a question that opens, e.g., *"Is there a fascinating project brewing in your mind?"*

## A.5 — What makes this NOT slop

The wave button. The 5-line loader. The asymmetric navbar with the vertical divider. The Flip-based filter (not opacity fade). The contact form pre-segmenting leads via service-interest checkboxes. **Without these specific moves, you've made another agency site.**

# § B — Solo Portfolio Template

**Reference:** marcelodesignx.com (the personal site behind the agency)
**Use for:** designer/dev personal site, single-operator brand, freelance portfolio
**Feeling defaults:** *intimacy, craft, hush*

## B.1 — Section order

1. Loader (lighter — 3-line cycling copy is fine, since the site is more personal)
2. Navbar — minimal: name/logo left, 2–3 links center, contact CTA right
3. Hero — first-person opener (`I [verb] [noun]`), portrait or self-rendered avatar to the right, asymmetric
4. Selected work — 3–5 case study cards in a vertical stack with offset layout (alternating left/right alignment)
5. About — long-form first-person paragraph, one quote pulled out in display type
6. Process / how-I-work numbered list (optional)
7. Now / currently — what you're working on right now, dated
8. Contact — single email link, large display type, no form
9. Footer — minimal, social row, year, location

## B.2 — Color & typography

- Base: `#070707` (pushes more cinematic than agency)
- Off-white: `#F0F0F0` (cool — feels editorial)
- Signal: personal-defining accent (a single hue tied to your work)
- Mute: `#7A7A7A`
- Sans: Geist Sans or General Sans
- Display: PP Editorial New (free for personal) — adds the editorial feel

## B.3 — Custom moments (use ≥3)

- ✦ Hero portrait that subtly tracks cursor (parallax depth, ±8px max)
- ✦ Case study cards with hover-reveal video preview
- ✦ Pull-quote in the about section animated via SplitText character reveal
- ✦ A "now" line that updates with real date — small detail that signals the site is alive
- ✦ Email link with character-roll hover

## B.4 — Copy archetype

- Hero: first-person and intimate. *"I design things you'll feel before you read."*
- Case studies: lead with the role, not the company. *"Lead designer · Drip Aqua · 2024"*
- About: written like a letter, not a bio. Use "I", not "He" or "We".
- Contact: a single line. *"Find me at hello@you.com"* — no form needed.
- Footer: location + year only. No mission statement.

## B.5 — What makes this NOT slop

The first-person voice throughout. The asymmetric stack instead of a grid. The "now" timestamp. The absence of a contact form. Solo portfolios fail when they imitate agency sites — this template forces personal scale.

# § C — Consumer Brand Product Template

**Reference:** Drip Aqua
**Use for:** beverage, apparel, beauty, lifestyle DTC, single-product brand
**Feeling defaults:** *coolness, wake, refreshment, charge*

## C.1 — Section order

1. Loader — branded counter, single short tagline (no 5-line cycling — keep it punchy)
2. Navbar — minimal: logo center or left, "Shop" CTA right, cart icon
3. Hero — product hero render (3D, not photography) at the visual center, headline as one bold sentence underneath, blur atmosphere matched to the product's hue
4. Ingredient / craft section — 3–4 visual cards each highlighting one component of the product
5. Texture / sensory beat — full-width video loop or animated render showing the product in motion (liquid, fabric drape, foundation swirl)
6. Lifestyle imagery beat — but NOT stock photos. Photoshoot or rendered scene that fits the feeling word.
7. Reviews / press / social proof — three short testimonials, max
8. Buy — clear price, variant selector, single primary CTA
9. Footer — quiet: legal, support email, social row, no ambitious one-liner

## C.2 — Color & typography

- Base: matches the product palette, not a default dark. (Drip Aqua leaned electric blue + ice white; an amber whiskey brand would lean warm cream + smoke charcoal.)
- Signal: a high-saturation pop of the product's defining color
- Mute: a temperature-matched neutral
- Sans: Aventa or General Sans
- Display: Cabinet Grotesk for the hero headline only — keeps it editorial without being too literary

## C.3 — Custom moments (use ≥3)

- ✦ Product render that **rotates slightly** as the cursor moves (parallax depth, looks 3D without being 3D)
- ✦ Ingredient cards that flip or expand on hover to reveal more detail
- ✦ Texture loop video at section 5 plays on scroll-into-view, pauses out
- ✦ Variant selector with character-roll labels
- ✦ Buy button with magnetic floating cursor target

## C.4 — Copy archetype

- Hero: short. ≤6 words. *"Made for the morning after."* / *"Still cold. Still here."*
- Ingredient section: one-word headline per card (`Magnesium`, `Electrolytes`, `Coconut`), with one short sentence beneath
- Sensory beat: no copy at all, or a single line at top
- Lifestyle: a single sentence per shot, not a paragraph
- Buy: just the price and variant — don't oversell
- Footer: bare. The product is the brand voice.

## C.5 — What makes this NOT slop

The product render is the hero, not a stock lifestyle photo. The ingredient cards have personality (not generic icons). The sensory beat plays. The buy section doesn't try to sell — it lets the visitor decide. **Banned from this template:** countdown timers, "as seen in" logos in row form, "join 50,000 customers" social proof bars.

# § D — B2B SaaS / Enterprise Template

**Reference:** 73 Strings
**Use for:** fintech platform, AI/ML SaaS, vertical SaaS for enterprise buyers
**Feeling defaults:** *gravity, stillness, authority, precision*

## D.1 — Section order

1. Loader — counter only, no cycling copy (enterprise buyers don't want whimsy in load states)
2. Navbar — full-width with utility links: Solutions · Industries · Resources · Customers, plus "Book a Demo" + "Contact" CTAs (two CTAs allowed in this archetype only)
3. Hero — bold headline, three-clause structure (Drip Aqua won't fit here; B2B benefits from longer hero copy). Background can be a subtle animated dataviz or chart skeleton, not a blur atmosphere.
4. Logo wall — quiet, single-row, named clients (only if you have them; skip if not)
5. Metrics row — 3 numbers (`99% accuracy · 10x faster · 50% cost reduction`) animated counters as they enter viewport
6. Solutions grid — 3–4 product modules, each linking to a deeper subpage
7. Customer story / case study spotlight — quote, customer logo, link to full study
8. Industries served — segmented pills (PE, VC, Infra) — clicking shows tailored value prop
9. CTA banner — "Book a Demo" full-width, single button
10. Footer — multi-column: Product · Solutions · Resources · Company · Legal · Offices (B2B has more footer real estate)

## D.2 — Color & typography

- Base: `#0A0A0A` or a deep navy `#0B1430`
- Off-white: `#F5F2EC` warm — softens the enterprise feel
- Signal: a precise, calibrated accent (electric blue, mint green, or burnt amber). **Avoid red — reads as "alert" in B2B contexts.**
- Mute: `#5B6871`
- Sans: Söhne or Neue Haas Grotesk (paid, but B2B clients usually have budget) — these faces signal seriousness
- Display: same sans at heavier weight; serif accents feel out of place in B2B unless the brand is explicitly heritage

## D.3 — Custom moments (use ≥3)

- ✦ Animated metric counters on viewport entry (count up from 0 to target value over 1.5s, eased "mdx")
- ✦ Industries pill row that crossfades the value-prop block as the user clicks each pill
- ✦ Hero background dataviz — animated lines or particles suggesting data flow, **subtle, not flashy**
- ✦ Customer logo wall with hover-state showing case-study link
- ✦ Demo CTA with magnetic floating cursor target

## D.4 — Copy archetype

- Hero: three clauses. *"Automated insights. Augmented strategies. Alpha unlocked."* (73 Strings' actual line — note the parallel structure and confident periods)
- Metrics: numbers, not adjectives. `99% accuracy` beats `industry-leading accuracy`.
- Solutions cards: one-line value prop per card, then a `Learn more →` link. No paragraphs in the grid.
- Customer story: lead with the quote. Logo and name underneath. Single line. *"73 Strings cut our valuation cycle from 6 weeks to 3 days." — Sarah K., Managing Director.*
- CTA banner: a single imperative. `Book a Demo`. Not "Get Started" or "Sign Up Today."

## D.5 — What makes this NOT slop

The metrics row uses real numbers, not adjectives. The industries pills are interactive, not just labels. The hero copy lands with three confident clauses, not a feature list. The footer has actual depth (this is one place where MDX's "minimal footer" rule is broken — enterprise sites benefit from sitemap-grade footers). **Banned from this template:** "AI-powered" anywhere, generic "innovate / transform / disrupt" copy, hero illustrations of people pointing at laptops, or any copy that sounds like it came from a startup pitch deck.

# § E — Creative Specialty Studio Template

**Reference:** Flight Path (animation/motion specialty studio)
**Use for:** niche craft studio — animation-only, sound-only, branding-only, photography-only
**Feeling defaults:** *charge, ignition, momentum, focus*

## E.1 — Section order

1. Loader — moody, atmospheric (longer than agency loader — 3s is fine here, the wait IS the brand)
2. Navbar — sparse: logo, one or two links (`Work` · `Studio`), maybe a `Reel ›` link
3. Hero — full viewport with a **looping signature reel** as the background, NOT a still. Headline overlays the reel. Reel autoplays muted, no controls visible.
4. Selected work — 3–6 large project cards, full-width or near-full-width. Each card hover plays its own preview reel.
5. Capabilities / craft list — single column, large display type, listed like a poem (one capability per line, NOT a grid)
6. Behind-the-scenes / studio beat — photography or rendered scene of the workspace, tools, process. One paragraph.
7. Clients / press strip — quiet, minimal
8. Contact — single email link in display type, no form
9. Footer — bare: studio name, year, location, social row

## E.2 — Color & typography

- Base: `#070707` — pushes deeper than agency template, more cinematic
- Off-white: `#F0F0F0` cool
- Signal: a single saturated accent that matches the studio's craft (motion studios often go acid green or electric magenta)
- Mute: `#7A7A7A`
- Sans: General Sans or Geist Sans
- Display: PP Neue Montreal (paid) or Cabinet Grotesk

## E.3 — Custom moments (use ≥3)

- ✦ Hero reel autoplays in background, muted. Headline split-text reveals over it on entry.
- ✦ Project cards each have their own preview reel that swaps in on hover (not a still-to-still fade — a fade-to-motion)
- ✦ Capabilities list with each line entering on its own scroll trigger, not stagger
- ✦ Cursor magnet on the email link in contact section
- ✦ A subtle CRT scanline or film-grain overlay on hero (only if your craft includes motion/film — skip otherwise)

## E.4 — Copy archetype

- Hero: ignition language. *"Frame zero starts here."* / *"Where the cut begins."*
- Capabilities list: each line a single noun or noun phrase. *Direction. Animation. Compositing. Color. Sound design.* — read like a poem.
- Project descriptions: short. One client name, one credit line. *"Nike · Air Max 1 launch · Direction & animation, 2024"*
- Contact: a verb-only invitation. *"Tell us what you're cutting."* — feels craft-aware.
- Footer: just facts. Studio name. City. Year.

## E.5 — What makes this NOT slop

The looping hero reel — not a still hero. The capabilities list as a poem, not a grid. The project cards that fade-to-motion on hover. The single email contact (no form — clients who book specialty studios don't fill out forms). **Banned from this template:** any "process diagram" with arrows between numbered steps, any "our values" section, any pricing tier grid.

# § F — FinTech / Capital / Wealth Template

**Reference:** Swanson Reserve Capital, Future Mortgage Landing
**Use for:** wealth management, lending, advisory firms, capital allocators, mortgage/loan products
**Feeling defaults:** *gravity, restraint, quiet authority*

This is distinct from § D (B2B SaaS): D sells **operational software**; F sells **trust and access**. The reader is not evaluating a feature set — they are evaluating whether they would let this firm hold money on their behalf. The site has to feel like an oak desk in a corner office, not a product launch.

## F.1 — Section order

1. Loader — counter only, no cycling copy. Wealth doesn't whimsy.
2. Navbar — full width, restrained: `Approach · Strategies · Insights · Contact`. No CTA button — a single underlined link `Schedule a Conversation` lives top-right.
3. Hero — quietly confident, NOT a headline pyrotechnic. Short clause, big breath of negative space, an editorial portrait or rendered architectural detail (column, marble, brushed brass) at low contrast.
4. Track record — three numbers (`$2.4B AUM · 38 yrs · 12 partners`), counter-up, restrained typography
5. Approach / philosophy beat — one paragraph in display type, anchored on a single sentence pulled out larger
6. Strategy / product breakdown — 2–4 named strategies (e.g., `Reserve Income`, `Generational Wealth`), each with a one-paragraph description, no icons
7. Leadership / partners — quiet portrait grid (1×4 or 2×2), no hover effects, names + titles only
8. Insights / publications — 2–3 most recent thought pieces, byline + date + 2-line excerpt
9. Contact — schedule-a-call form with calendar embed, OR a single email + phone line in display type (depending on access tier)
10. Footer — sitemap-style, license/registration disclosures in small caps, FINRA/SEC links, office addresses

## F.2 — Color & typography

- Base: `#0B1430` (deep navy — not black, navy reads as institutional)
- Off-white: `#F5F2EC` (warm) or `#E8E4D9` (parchment)
- Signal: `#B08D57` (warm bronze) or `#3A6B7A` (deep teal) — never gold (gold reads "luxury consumer," wrong for capital)
- Mute: `#5B6470`
- Sans: Söhne (paid) or Geist Sans
- Display: Tobias (paid) or PP Editorial New — serif weight is mandatory; capital firms without serif anchors feel scammy

## F.3 — Custom moments (use ≥3)

- ✦ Track-record counters that animate slowly (3s, eased `mdx-still` not `mdx`) — speed conveys volume, slowness conveys mass
- ✦ Architectural detail render parallax — column, marble, brass — drifts ±4px (less than consumer parallax) on cursor move
- ✦ Pull-quote in approach section, set in display serif, animated via clip-mask reveal on scroll
- ✦ "Schedule a Conversation" link with character-roll hover (the ONE place to use kinetic micro-interaction)
- ✦ Section dividers are single hairlines (1px, mute color), full-width, animated to draw left-to-right on scroll-into-view

## F.4 — Copy archetype

- Hero: a posture, not a pitch. *"We've held wealth carefully since 1987."* / *"Capital, well kept."*
- Track record: numbers without adjectives. `$2.4B in client assets` — never `over $2 billion in assets under management.`
- Approach: written in third-person institutional voice. *"The firm believes that..."* not *"We believe..."*
- Strategy descriptions: one paragraph, no bullets. Bullets feel transactional in capital.
- Leadership: name, title, one credential line. Never a chatty bio.
- Insights titles: declarative, often dateline-style. *"The case for income, 2026."*
- Contact: an invitation, not a CTA. *"For new mandates, reach Maria Chen at..."*

## F.5 — What makes this NOT slop

The serif anchor. The slow counters (slow because the assets are heavy). The single hairline dividers instead of section background changes. The third-person institutional voice. The phone number on the contact section (capital firms whose only contact is a form feel like they don't have real clients). **Banned from this template:** stock imagery of handshakes, "trusted by 10,000+ investors" social proof bars, gradient backgrounds, animated coin/dollar particles, anything that gestures at "modern fintech" — the brand needs to feel older than fintech.

# § G — Web3 / Crypto Product Template

**Reference:** Cirus Web3 Wallet, Fedorgost
**Use for:** wallets, on-chain trading apps, dapps, L2 protocols, crypto custody products
**Feeling defaults:** *charge, future-tense, frictionless motion*

This template is the opposite of § F. Where capital wants gravity, Web3 wants velocity. But it must avoid the meme-coin / Solana-summer slop look — neon Comic Sans, pixel mascots, retail-bait gradients. Marcelo's Web3 work threads a specific needle: futurist but expensive.

## G.1 — Section order

1. Loader — counter + a single 3D rendered loop in the background (token, coin, abstract geometry rotating)
2. Navbar — minimal: logo, `Wallet · Earn · Developers · Docs`, primary CTA `Launch App ›` (the link goes to a separate dapp subdomain)
3. Hero — full-bleed 3D render of the product (wallet interface mockup, abstract geometry, holographic coin), headline overlaid on it. Headline is short, future-tense.
4. Live data strip — TVL, total holders, networks supported, animated tickers (this is where Web3 sites earn legitimacy)
5. Capability beats — 3 sections, each a full viewport: send / receive / earn, OR trade / stake / bridge. Each has its own short headline + a 3D animation or interactive mockup.
6. Network/integrations strip — chain logos, partner logos in a quiet row
7. Security beat — audits (Certik, Trail of Bits, OpenZeppelin), bug bounty, open-source repo links
8. Developer / docs CTA — secondary path for builders
9. Footer — links to docs, GitHub, Discord, X, governance forum, legal disclaimer about crypto risks

## G.2 — Color & typography

- Base: `#06070C` (true deep — Web3 sites lean darker than agency)
- Off-white: `#EAECF1` (cool, slightly blue-shifted)
- Signal: a single saturated future-tense hue — electric purple `#7C5CFF`, holographic green `#00E5A0`, or signal cyan `#00BFFF`. **Never** orange/yellow (looks like Coinbase/Binance retail).
- Mute: `#4B5263`
- Sans: General Sans (Fontshare) — geometric, legible at all sizes
- Display: NB International Pro (paid) or Aeonik (paid) — both feel "system-future." Avoid serifs entirely in this template.

## G.3 — Custom moments (use ≥3)

- ✦ Hero 3D render of product/coin/geometry, slow rotation (one full rotation / 30s) + cursor-driven tilt parallax
- ✦ Live data tickers in the legitimacy strip — actual values via API, animating on update (TVL, holders, etc.)
- ✦ Capability sections each have their own bespoke 3D scene — render once, ship as WebP/WebM loop, never live three.js
- ✦ "Launch App ›" CTA with a subtle holographic glow on hover (use a CSS filter, not a static box-shadow)
- ✦ Background grid or particle field that drifts — NEVER stops moving (charge feeling word demands continuous motion)

## G.4 — Copy archetype

- Hero: short, future-tense, often a verb-noun pair. *"Self-custody, simplified."* / *"Send. Earn. Settle."*
- Live data: numbers with units, no adjectives. `$1.2B TVL · 4 chains · 280K wallets`
- Capability section headlines: imperative-mode verbs. *"Hold what's yours."* / *"Earn while you sleep."* / *"Bridge in one click."*
- Security copy: factual, third-party-anchored. *"Audited by Certik, May 2025"* — link to the report, never asterisk it.
- Developer CTA: technical and direct. *"Read the docs ›"* never *"Build the future with us."*
- Footer disclaimer: regulatory, sober — *"Cryptocurrencies carry significant risk. Past performance is not indicative of future results."*

## G.5 — What makes this NOT slop

The single saturated signal color (one, not five). The slow 3D rotations (slowness = expensive, fast spin = retail). The actual API-driven data tickers (not hardcoded numbers). The audit citations linking to real reports. The dev CTA pulled out as an explicit secondary path. **Banned from this template:** any animated coin with eyes, any "🚀" anywhere, anything that uses "moon" / "ape" / "WAGMI" / "GM" tonally, gradient-heavy hero backgrounds, multi-color rainbow anything, blob-shaped 3D glass orbs (the 2024 default), centered emoji-shaped logos.

# § H — AI / Data Product Landing Template

**Reference:** AI Landing Page concept (Behance)
**Use for:** single-product AI tool launches, demo-led landing pages, vertical AI applications, B2C generative AI products
**Feeling defaults:** *clarity, demonstration, calm capability*

Distinct from § D (B2B SaaS): D sells a platform with multiple modules; H sells **one thing that does one thing impressively well**. The page exists to make a visitor try the product or sign up for a demo within 90 seconds. It is structurally simpler than every other template.

## H.1 — Section order

1. Loader — minimal, ~1.5s. Don't make an AI product wait — feels like the AI is slow.
2. Navbar — sparse: logo, `Pricing · Docs`, CTA `Try it free ›` (or `Get demo ›`)
3. Hero — split layout: short benefit headline left, **live interactive demo right**. The demo IS the hero. No marketing illustration of "AI doing things" — show the actual UI doing actual work.
4. How it works — 3 steps, each a single sentence + a small UI screenshot or animation
5. Output gallery — 6–9 examples of what the AI produces. Real outputs, not curated marketing renders. Hover plays/expands each.
6. Use cases — 3–4 named verticals (`For marketers`, `For engineers`, `For analysts`) with one concrete example per
7. Pricing — clean tiered table OR a single "Free / Pro" toggle. No "Contact us" tier on H — that's § D territory.
8. Trust strip — security/privacy callouts: SOC 2, data retention policy, model used, who has access
9. Final CTA — same as nav CTA, repeated big
10. Footer — minimal: docs, privacy, terms, status page, contact

## H.2 — Color & typography

- Base: `#0F1115` (slightly cooler than agency dark — feels system-like)
- Off-white: `#F4F5F7`
- Signal: a confident technical hue — `#6366F1` (indigo), `#06B6D4` (cyan), or `#10B981` (emerald). **Avoid** purple-pink gradients (the 2023–24 generic AI default).
- Mute: `#6B7280`
- Sans: Geist Sans or Söhne — Geist is best because it was designed for Vercel and reads "AI-native" without trying
- Display: rarely used — display type in AI templates often signals "more marketing than product." Stick to weight contrast within the sans family.

## H.3 — Custom moments (use ≥3)

- ✦ **Live demo in hero** — the actual product, not a screenshot. Pre-fill it with a default input that runs in 2s, so the visitor sees output before they touch anything.
- ✦ Output gallery items expand on hover/click to show full result
- ✦ "Run it" buttons inline in the page that trigger live API calls (rate-limited) — the visitor experiences the product, not a description
- ✦ Pricing toggle (monthly/yearly) with smooth number animation when switched
- ✦ Code-block "Try via API" panel with copy-to-clipboard and language tabs (Python / curl / TS) — even on consumer products this signals seriousness

## H.4 — Copy archetype

- Hero: benefit, not feature. *"Caption a year of meetings in 30 seconds."* not *"AI-powered transcription"*. Be specific about input → output.
- How it works: 3 sentences max. *"Paste a link. Pick a tone. Get a draft."*
- Output gallery: each item has a one-line caption with the prompt or input that produced it. No marketing language.
- Use cases: a verb sentence per persona. *"For marketers: turn one launch post into a week of content."*
- Pricing: prices visible. *"$0 forever · $19/mo · custom"* — never "Contact for pricing" outside enterprise tier.
- CTA: action verbs only. `Try it free`, `Run a sample`, `See it in action`. Never `Learn more`.

## H.5 — What makes this NOT slop

The live demo in the hero (this single move beats every "AI-powered platform that revolutionizes..." headline). Real outputs in the gallery, not staged ones. Inline code samples. Visible pricing. Specific verbs in the hero (caption / draft / generate / classify) instead of "AI-powered." **Banned from this template:** the purple-pink gradient hero (industry default — looks generic now), animated brain illustrations, any "neural" / "intelligent" / "smart" adjective, "powered by GPT-4" callouts (date themselves quickly), "the future of [X]" copy. If the model name is the differentiator, you don't have a product yet.

# § I — Sports / Energy / Performance Template

**Reference:** Nike Landing concept, Red Bull Website concept, Logitech Landing concept
**Use for:** athletic apparel/footwear, energy drinks, performance hardware, esports, fitness tech, automotive performance
**Feeling defaults:** *kinetic, ignition, charge, ground-shake*

This is the opposite of § C (Drip Aqua) on the same axis. Drip Aqua is consumer-quiet; § I is consumer-loud. Where Drip Aqua wants a still product render and a sip of negative space, § I wants motion at every frame — athletes mid-stride, splash dynamics, drop shadows that suggest mass, motion blur that suggests speed. Marcelo's spec work for Nike and Red Bull on Behance reveals the formula.

## I.1 — Section order

1. Loader — kinetic from frame one. Counter accelerates non-linearly (slow start, fast finish — eased `expo.in`). Background: animated grid lines or motion streaks.
2. Navbar — bold, can be full-bleed, often with the brand wordmark large-left and an energetic CTA right (`Shop the drop`, `Watch the launch`)
3. Hero — full-viewport video loop. Athlete in motion, product in motion, or branded environmental motion (cars, motorcycles, skate, dance). Headline overlay short, dropped low or low-right, NOT centered.
4. Product / drop reveal — large product image with one rotational interaction, or a video loop of the product in use
5. Specs / features — laid out as a stat block (`230g · 13mm stack · React foam`) — sports buyers shop on numbers
6. Athlete / community spotlight — real photography, action shot, named athlete or community figure with a quote
7. Drop / story / lookbook — editorial-grade imagery in an alternating asymmetric grid
8. Buy / configure — variants, sizes, colorways with hover-swap product imagery
9. Footer — energetic but tight: brand wordmark big again, social row, returns/shipping policy

## I.2 — Color & typography

- Base: brand-defined. Sports brands often own a palette (Nike orange + black, Red Bull red+blue+silver). Don't override.
- Off-white: `#F5F5F2` cool concrete
- Signal: brand-defining accent (high saturation, used liberally — sports breaks the ≤3% rule. Charge feels like color saturation.)
- Mute: industrial grey
- Sans: a wide industrial face — Helvetica Now (paid), Suisse Int'l (paid), or for free: Inter Display, Geist Sans at the boldest weights. Sports is one of the few categories where Inter is allowed (because the brand is loud enough to override the typeface neutrality).
- Display: NEUE MACHINA (paid) or BLT (Behance freebies) — extended condensed faces feel athletic.

## I.3 — Custom moments (use ≥3)

- ✦ **Hero video loop**, NOT a still. Loop must contain an instant of stillness mid-motion (an athlete frozen at peak jump) — that's what gets remembered.
- ✦ Product reveal with cursor-driven 3D rotation (sports buyers want to inspect, not just admire)
- ✦ Spec block where each number animates in sequentially (count-up, eased `expo.out`)
- ✦ Athlete quote with name + sport + signature in a hand-script font (the only place script fonts are allowed in MDX work)
- ✦ Lookbook with cursor-driven panning — the visitor "walks through" the imagery rather than scrolling past it

## I.4 — Copy archetype

- Hero: short, imperative, often verb-only. *"Run further."* / *"Drop everything."* / *"Made to move."*
- Specs: numbers with units. `230g · 13mm stack · React foam · 4mm drop`
- Athlete quote: first-person, present tense, unflowery. *"I race in these because they don't lie."* — Sha'Carri Richardson, sprinter.
- Drop story: editorial, two paragraphs max, can be poetic
- Buy: minimal — colorway names, size selector, single button. Confidence through brevity.
- Footer: brand wordmark or motto, no sentimentality

## I.5 — What makes this NOT slop

The hero video loop containing a moment of stillness (everyone uses video; the moment of stillness is what differentiates). Real athlete photography, not stock. Specs as a stat block. Brand palette respected, not overridden. The script-font quote. **Banned from this template:** stock fitness photos, "the future of performance," any "level up" / "unlock your potential" copy, any photo where the athlete is mid-laugh in a non-context outfit, gradient backgrounds (sports doesn't gradient — it impacts).

# § J — Multi-SKU Fashion / Lifestyle E-Commerce Template

**Reference:** Lapin e-Commerce
**Use for:** fashion brands, beauty catalogs, home goods, multi-product lifestyle retailers
**Feeling defaults:** *curation, taste, slow browsing*

Distinct from § C (single-product brand): C launches one hero product; J operates a storefront with 30–500 SKUs across categories. The feel must be **edited gallery, not warehouse**. Lapin is the reference because it solves the multi-SKU layout problem without falling into Shopify-default grid slop.

## J.1 — Section order

1. Loader — light, one-line of copy ("Selecting today's pieces"), 1.5s
2. Navbar — restrained: logo center, `Shop · Edit · Journal · Account`, cart icon top-right, search icon
3. Hero — editorial-scale photograph (full bleed) with a single short headline overlaid. NOT a "shop now" hero. Closer to Vogue cover than Amazon.
4. Featured collection / curation — 3–5 hand-picked products laid out asymmetrically (offset cards, varying sizes — never a uniform grid in section 4)
5. Category navigation — large-type category links (`OUTERWEAR ›`, `KNITS ›`, `ACCESSORIES ›`) with hover-reveal of one representative image per category. Editorial feel.
6. Editorial / brand story — a short piece, image + 2 paragraphs. Functions as the "About" without being labeled About.
7. New arrivals strip — horizontal scroll row of 8–12 newest items, each card has hover-reveal of secondary image
8. Customer / press strip — a quote or two from press; quiet, not braggy
9. Newsletter signup — single field, single line of copy. *"For early access and the occasional letter."*
10. Footer — sitemap-style: Shop / Customer Care / Company / Legal / Country selector

## J.2 — Color & typography

- Base: brand-defined, often warm cream `#F5F1EA` or cool ivory `#F6F6F4` — dark fashion sites are rarer (only for streetwear / luxury edge)
- Off-white: secondary tone within 5–10% of base
- Signal: ONE brand color, used on price tags, sale labels, and the cart count badge
- Mute: a refined grey for body and metadata (`#7A7570`)
- Sans: General Sans, Söhne — anything contemporary-editorial
- Display: Tobias, PP Editorial New, Tiempos (paid) — fashion almost always wants a serif. The serif IS the brand voice for half of fashion sites.

## J.3 — Custom moments (use ≥3)

- ✦ Asymmetric featured-collection layout — offset cards, varying widths, deliberate negative space. NEVER a uniform 3- or 4-column grid in this section.
- ✦ Category nav hover-reveal — hovering on a category name reveals a single representative product image to the side, full-color
- ✦ Product card hover swaps to secondary image (front-of-garment → back-of-garment, or product → product-on-model)
- ✦ Newsletter input that gets the character-roll hover only on the submit button
- ✦ Country selector in footer — animated drawer or modal, NOT a default `<select>`. Tiny detail, signals globalization seriousness.

## J.4 — Copy archetype

- Hero: editorial, single sentence, often a season name or a posture. *"Spring 2026 — Quiet hours."* / *"Made for the pause between seasons."*
- Category names: ALL CAPS, single-word when possible, with a chevron `›`
- Product titles: descriptive but short. *"Linen tunic in oat"* — never *"100% Premium European Linen Loose Fit Designer Tunic"*. (Title bloat is Amazon-tier slop.)
- Editorial section: written like a magazine column, present-tense, observational
- Press quotes: minimal attribution. *"A masterclass in restraint." — VOGUE*
- Newsletter: one-line invitation, no benefit bullets

## J.5 — What makes this NOT slop

The asymmetric featured collection (uniform grids are the Shopify-default failure mode). Editorial copy in the hero — fashion that talks like Amazon dies on contact. The category hover-reveal of representative imagery. Short, descriptive product titles. Country selector with care. **Banned from this template:** "Shop Now" / "Buy Now" buttons (use product card itself as the click target), "trending" / "best-seller" badges (they cheapen), countdown timers, "limited stock!" urgency manipulation, model carousels in the hero that auto-advance, hero videos of someone twirling in a dress (overused).

# Choosing between archetypes

If you're stuck, walk this decision tree top-down — first match wins:

1. **Is the site for one person (solo designer / dev / consultant)?** → § B Solo Portfolio
2. **Does the company hold money or extend credit?** (wealth, lending, advisory, capital firm) → § F FinTech / Capital
3. **Is the product on-chain / a wallet / a dapp / a protocol?** → § G Web3 / Crypto
4. **Is the entire pitch a single AI tool with a live demo?** → § H AI / Data Product Landing
5. **Is the brand sports / energy / performance, with athletes or motion at its core?** → § I Sports / Performance
6. **Does the site sell a multi-SKU catalog (apparel, beauty, home, lifestyle)?** → § J Multi-SKU E-Commerce
7. **Does it sell ONE hero physical product (beverage, gadget, single-product DTC)?** → § C Consumer Brand
8. **Is the company an operational B2B SaaS platform (not a financial firm)?** → § D B2B SaaS
9. **Is the company a niche craft studio (animation-only, sound-only, photography-only)?** → § E Creative Specialty
10. **Default for any multi-service creative agency / dev shop / studio:** → § A Agency

## Common confusions

- **§ D vs § F:** 73 Strings (D) sells *valuation software* — the buyer evaluates features. Swanson Reserve (F) sells *access to advisors* — the buyer evaluates trust. If the visitor would ask "what does it do?" → D. If "who are these people?" → F.
- **§ C vs § J:** Drip Aqua (C) is one product launching. Lapin (J) is a storefront with 50+ SKUs. If the homepage's job is to make one item famous → C. If the homepage's job is to curate browsing → J.
- **§ C vs § I:** Both can be physical product. C wants stillness (single product, hush). I wants kinetics (motion, athletes, charge). The product determines: a $58 morning supplement = C; a $180 running shoe = I.
- **§ A vs § E:** Agency (A) is multi-service. Specialty (E) does one craft. If the work spans UI/UX + dev + branding + 3D → A. If it's animation-only or sound-only → E.
- **§ G vs § H:** Both can feel "future-tense." G has on-chain money flowing; H runs ML inference. If a wallet connects → G. If text-in / text-out (or image-in / image-out) → H.

**Rule of thumb:** if you can't decide between two archetypes, the smaller one almost always wins. MDX sites get worse when they over-scope. A solo founder shouldn't ship the agency template even if they have multiple service lines.

# When to deviate from a template

The templates are starting points. You'll deviate when:

- The feeling word demands a different rhythm (e.g., feeling word is `hush` → drop the loader length, drop the showreel, lengthen the negative space)
- The brand has a structural asset the template doesn't anticipate (e.g., a beverage with a real ritual — pour, sip, cool — earns a dedicated section)
- The copy archetype clashes with the actual brand voice (rare — usually the template wins)

Deviation is **always intentional**, never accidental. If you find yourself "improving" a template by adding sections, you're probably about to ship slop. Cut, don't add.
